# Repository Maintenance

Read README.md, docs/editorial-policy.md and data/README.md. This is a knowledge repository: maintain notes and structured data, not a long-lived collector or service.

Preserve sources, dates and limitations. Never claim a person completed a reading from an AI recommendation. Exclude private plans, personal feedback, credentials, absolute paths, PDFs, raw article text and logs. Update indexes, deduplicate identifiers and validate JSON/CSV/local links before committing. Do not change visibility automatically. No empty, backdated or timestamp-only commits.

Preserve the reader-facing README structure: purpose, audience and use cases, topic landscape, selected sources, reusable data, reading steps, and evidence policy. Update latest links and the collection-stats block when the collection changes; do not replace the homepage with a short operational summary.

Keep README.md as the full English homepage and README.zh-CN.md as its complete Simplified Chinese counterpart. Show reciprocal English / 简体中文 links at the top of both files. Keep positioning, sections, source links, latest entries, and collection-stats counts and dates consistent in both languages whenever content changes. Preserve both collection-stats marker blocks. State that most research notes and supporting guides are currently in Chinese; do not imply that the whole archive is translated. Never replace the English default with Chinese during routine updates.

Maintain an Updates section in both homepages, using updates-daily and updates-milestones marker blocks. Show at most the last 7 calendar days of daily entries (Asia/Shanghai), newest first, one short line per date with concrete counts, direct links, and one plain-language topic highlight; retain at most 3 meaningful milestones separately. Archive all older entries in CHANGELOG.md and CHANGELOG.zh-CN.md with reciprocal language links. Historical backfill follows existing material dates and is explicitly labeled as backfilled on 2026-10-10, the repository launch date; never present those dates as earlier GitHub publication or invent activity for missing dates. Future entries use the actual publication date and substantive git diff: distinguish new unique resources, revisions, and backfilled issues; group reruns on the same date and never count repeat appearances as new papers. Keep English/Chinese dates, counts, and links aligned. Use plain language, a few small emoji, and no empty daily entries or timestamp-only commits. Only record successful publication; a pending entry must be committed with the corresponding substantive change, and failed pushes must not advance local publication state.
